# My Integrity Reflection

Within my mini cluster, replication helped protect against data loss when my node 2 failed because there were two more copies of each of the lost blocks across the other nodes. Within node 2, blocks 1, 2, 4, and 5 were completely deleted. Originally, I planned on just deleting a couple of the blocks or deleting the data within the blocks, but decided to just delete them all to show how fault-tolerant this system is due to the replication factor of 3.

Within my data lake, partitioning my data based on year and month helped support faster analytics because the queries could just specify the year and month the data they needed was from. Even though my data was only from the Spring semester of 2026, as the database grows, this will become more helpful for looking at just the years. Additionally, even more partitioning could help make the queries faster and increase productivity. For example, if I were to go back and add more data to simulate the database growing, I would also partition based on semester (Spring and Fall) to help the queries run faster and avoid scanning irrelevant or outdated data.

To scale up this data lake to a real university data lake, I would make the change to partition based on year, semester, and month. This would help the queries run faster and skip over irrelevant data as the lake grows. Additionally, I would add partitions for raw and curated data. This way, the queries can be run on data that has been cleaned and is the most accurate. The raw data could also be accessed if needed. I would also add validation steps and access controls to help preserve the integrity of the data lake.
